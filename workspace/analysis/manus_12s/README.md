## Audio and editorial reading

Reference-window RMS ranges 0.115–0.254; the measured maximum is in 156.0–166.5s. Spectral-flux novelty candidates range from 30 to 60 per window. The three-band mean-power comparison is strongly dominated by the low band throughout; this does not mean that the original music is perceived as bass-only. It is not a stem separation. The reference’s transferable editorial quality is contrast between clean typographic breaths and readable product proof; chapter changes vary framing and register without maintaining a permanently dense overlay. Use the separately analyzed Life Force beat map—not this unrelated reference soundtrack—for NewEra edit timing.

## Limits and transfer to NewEra

Sparse stills and coarse signal summaries indicate visual/state changes and audio activity; they do not establish exact cut boundaries, motion curves, perceived loudness, semantic contents between samples, or sync tolerance. Spectral-flux events are novelty candidates, not verified musical beats.

The 14 contact sheets cover the full reference duration, with the final partial window. Sparse frames may miss brief cuts and text. Neither the image delta nor flux candidates establish cuts per minute/bar, exact transient-to-frame error, velocity/easing, perceived loudness, or precise musical events. NewEra takes only broad design principles: high-contrast chapter punctuation, one clear proof at a time, restrained accents, and a quieter resolved finish. No Manus footage, logos, copy, characters, or UI assets are used.


## Dense full-duration signal-level supplement

`../manus_frame_events/analysis.json` and its CSV companions add reproducible measurements for the complete 166.464 s reference. `analyze_manus_frame_events.py` asks FFmpeg `scdet` for a score on every decoded 30 fps frame (4,993 frames), then analyzes mono audio at 22,050 Hz using a 2,048-point STFT and 441-sample hop (20 ms). The per-frame file contains 4,993 observations; the audio feature file contains 8,319 centered STFT observations, spanning approximately 0.046–166.406 s. It includes RMS, positive spectral flux, and coarse band-power features (20–120, 120–250, 250–2,500, and 4,000 Hz to Nyquist).

At score thresholds 5/10/15/20, the detector flagged 86/45/40/27 frame candidates, respectively; a separate local-maxima setting yielded 74 candidates. Global-threshold spectral-flux peak finding yielded 305 audio candidates. These are machine measurements only: visual scores may respond to flashes, motion, dissolves or compression, and flux peaks are not verified beats or instrument labels. No frame/audio event matching, cut-rate, bar grid, motion-curve, loudness-perception, or sync-tolerance claim is inferred. The 12-second visual narrative in the table remains based on sparse 1.5-second thumbnails. See the JSON `limitations` field and script for exact definitions and reproduction. Candidates require frame-by-frame and listening review before editorial use.
